What an open ball actually tells us about a point

A neighbourhood is local permission: how far may we move from a point and remain close?

By Dr. Vishwa Prakash Jha11 August 20265 min read

On this page

DefinitionWhat it meansInterior pointsThe metric matters

The notation B(x,r) is compact, but its meaning is geometric: collect every point whose distance from x is less than r. It tells us what “near x” means in the metric space under consideration.

The formal definition

Let (X,d) be a metric space. For x∈X and r>0, the open ball with centre x and radius r is

B(x,r) = { y∈X : d(x,y)<r }

The strict inequality is responsible for the word “open” in the name. Points at distance exactly r are not included.

The ball lies entirely inside E, so x has room to move without leaving E.

What does the ball tell us?

It specifies a controlled neighbourhood of x. Any point y in the ball is guaranteed to be closer than r to x. The ball says nothing about points beyond this radius, and it does not require the space to look Euclidean.

In ℝ with the usual distance d(x,y)=|x−y|, an open ball is simply an open interval:

B(x,r) = (x−r,x+r)

In ℝ² with Euclidean distance, it is the inside of a circle without the boundary circle.

Open balls detect interior points

A point x is an interior point of E if there is some radius r>0 such that B(x,r) is contained in E.

x∈E° ⇔ ∃r>0 such that B(x,r)⊆E

The radius is evidence that x is not pressed against the boundary. It has a positive amount of “breathing room” inside the set.

Mental picture

To test whether x is interior, ask: can I draw some open ball around x that stays completely inside E?

The metric changes the shape, not the role

The word “ball” does not promise a round Euclidean object. Under the taxicab metric in ℝ², balls appear diamond-shaped. Under the maximum metric, they appear square. In a discrete metric, sufficiently small balls may contain only their centre.

What remains unchanged is the logical role: a ball collects points satisfying a distance inequality. Topology uses these balls to define open sets, convergence, continuity, closure, and compactness.

One-line takeaway

An open ball translates the abstract metric into a usable local neighbourhood around a point.
